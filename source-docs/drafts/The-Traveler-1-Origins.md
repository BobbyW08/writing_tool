"The sea", his father said, not even bothering to look up from his nets. "The sea, the sea. Always the sea." He continued grumbling as he spooled his nets. Eron did not care if no one heard him. He did not want to have this conversation again.

Sero had learned this would not change his father's mind, but that would not stop him from carrying on anyway.

"I will not listen to these oafs argue over copper pieces all day."

"Son, you will do this. It has already been decided. These decisions are not made lightly. It is what is best for the future of our family."

Eron walked to Sero. Carrying his nets, bundled in his arms. He stood next to Sero, facing the opposite direction, and put his free hand on his son's shoulder. As Eron began to perhaps offer some comforting words, Sero sharply pushed his father's hand off his shoulder, turned from the docks, and walked away.

In that moment, Sero wanted nothing more than to carry on walking for centuries to come, never having to work in that musty shop with miserable Menas. As Sero proceeded from the docks, he trudged up the same hill he had been climbing his entire life. The town had grown from this particular road that led from the docks straight through town. It's rumored that when this continent was first discovered by settlers, they landed here first. Making Renmark one of the oldest cities known to humankind. Now as time has gone on, that one road is merely the center of a rapidly expanding territory that soon may need to be called a city.

Well they would need walls for that right? Wait, what makes a city a city and a town, a town?
